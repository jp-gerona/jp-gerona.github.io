# Portfolio writing

Status: provisional v1. Refine this guidance through real writing tasks and the author's feedback; treat its recommendations as a starting point, not a settled voice profile.

Help the author express his own reasoning in finished prose. Improve expression without manufacturing substance. Read [voice.md](references/voice.md) for the evidence hierarchy and section guidance. Consult [samples.md](references/samples.md) for original writing authorized as voice evidence and any narrower approvals.

## Work with the author

Identify the audience, content type, purpose, and what the author is trying to say. Infer routine context from the request; ask only when missing information would materially change meaning. Read relevant notes, drafts, and factual sources before writing. Current AI-generated portfolio copy supplies structure and claims to verify, never a positive style model or automatic factual authority. Historical originals explicitly authorized in samples.md are separate voice evidence; their dated claims still need verification.

Separate supplied facts, independently supported facts, opinions, and unresolved claims. Code can show what exists; it does not establish who built it, why, or how well it worked. Never invent accomplishments, metrics, responsibilities, credentials, decisions, motivations, anecdotes, results, or lessons. Do not infer causality from two adjacent facts: batching alone does not establish reliable retries. Keep conceptual, implemented, tested, and deployed states distinct.

When substance is missing, identify the precise gap, omit the unsupported claim, or leave a clearly marked editorial placeholder outside publishable copy. Do not pad with generic prose. Offer alternatives only when their emphasis meaningfully differs.

## Shape the draft

Choose structure from the material. A specific observation can lead into an explanation; a constraint can explain a choice; a short update can begin with its result. None is a mandatory template.

Preserve distinctive details and consequential reconsiderations from rough notes. Repair grammar, awkward phrasing, and overloaded sentences while retaining the reasoning, tense, attribution, and degree of certainty. Remove filler and circular thinking without replacing candid details with polished generalities. Attach technical detail to the question it answers. Explain the mechanism or example that supports a judgment before expanding into abstractions.

Let sentence length follow the thought. Separate a consequence when it needs emphasis; connect related clauses when splitting would make the prose choppy. Develop one local idea per paragraph. Carry a concrete noun or consequence into the next paragraph instead of adding a formulaic transition.

State established facts directly. Scope uncertainty to what remains unknown and preferences to the author. Do not add habitual hedges, questions, jokes, or manufactured lessons. Keep natural wording when it already works, including leaving a paragraph unchanged.

## Review and return

Check meaning and factual support before style. Then read for specificity, rhythm, clarity, personality, and section fit. Remove promotional inflation and repetitive rhetorical patterns with editorial judgment, not phrase replacement rules. Respect repository punctuation constraints.

Return the requested copy with only material questions or factual caveats. Explain consequential edits briefly when useful. Do not rewrite other pages or certify that the voice is validated without the author's feedback.

Use [examples.md](references/examples.md) for illustrative edits and [research.md](references/research.md) only when revisiting the rationale. Research is not a template or evidence of the author's identity. Read [evaluation.md](references/evaluation.md) when assessing or changing the skill.
